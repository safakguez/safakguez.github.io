Atlas Zoom in geography lessons

Atlas Zoom is a geography quiz for all 195 countries: flags and outlines under the magnifier, capitals and big cities, neighbouring countries and “Where is it?” right on the world map. Under “Learn → One country” it adds the main cities, rivers and mountains of each country. A learning mode with spaced repetition brings mistakes back soon and what students know only after days. 14 languages, no account.

How to use Atlas Zoom in class

Atlas Zoom is free for Android and iPhone/iPad. The apps show a short ad break now and then, never during a question. A one-time purchase (€0.99 or local equivalent) removes the ads.

Your school doesn't allow apps with ads?

Then there is a school edition in the browser for laptops, Chromebooks, tablets and interactive whiteboards. It has no ads, no purchases and no accounts, and you open it with a school code:

  • Request a free code by emailing contact@decly.app (your name, school and country are enough).
  • You get a class link (…/atlaszoom/schule/?code=…). The browser remembers the code. After that, the game starts straight away.
  • The code is valid until the end of the school year (31 July) and can be extended at any time.
  • Progress and profile stay in the browser of each device only. There is no sign-in, no data is sent to us and there is no analytics.

Lesson ideas

1. Starter: Trip of the Day (5–10 minutes)

Each day the trip makes 8 stops along real land borders, and it is the same for everyone. Play it together on the whiteboard or individually, then ask: Which borders did we cross? Where was the biggest jump, and why did we have to fly there (islands)?

2. “Where is it?” – estimating location and distance

Students tap a country on the world map and see how many kilometres they were off. Ideas: record and compare estimates; use an atlas to explain why countries in Europe are easier to hit than in Oceania; discuss why areas look different in size on maps (map projections).

3. Master one region in two weeks

In the magnifier quiz and in learning mode you can choose a region (for example Africa or South America). Five minutes a day are enough. Spaced repetition focuses on mistakes, and under “My Atlas” the countries students have learnt turn gold, so they can see their progress on the map.

4. Country profile with “One country”

For presentations or pair work: under “Learn → One country” the app shows a country's largest cities, rivers and mountains on its outline. Students add to the map with an atlas, for example climate, neighbours or economy.

Good to know

  • Countries: the 193 UN member states plus Vatican City and Palestine. Maps show internationally recognised borders (e.g. Crimea as part of Ukraine, Northern Cyprus as part of Cyprus, Somaliland as part of Somalia). Where there is no internationally recognised line (Kashmir, Southern Kurils), they follow the de facto administration; Taiwan is shown as its own area but is not a quiz country. This can be a good starting point for discussion.
  • Data: Natural Earth, Wikidata and world-countries (sources on the home page). Please report errors to contact@decly.app.
  • Atlas Zoom was developed with the help of AI (code, text, translations). The developer checks the content and data.
